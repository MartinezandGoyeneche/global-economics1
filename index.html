The Gender Wage Gap in Colombia: A Statistical Analysis Using Hypothesis Testing

The Gender Wage Gap in Colombia: A Statistical Analysis Using Hypothesis Testing

Sara Martínez & Mariana Goyeneche · Global Economics · Grade 11 · Term 3 · 2025–2026
Teacher: Nicolás López Cuéllar

Introduction

Economic inequality between genders remains one of the most persistent structural challenges in labor markets worldwide, and Colombia is no exception. Despite legal frameworks guaranteeing equal pay for equal work, empirical data consistently shows that female workers earn systematically less than their male counterparts across comparable occupations and qualification levels (DANE, 2023). Understanding whether this gap is statistically significant — rather than attributable to random sampling variation — is essential for evidence-based policy design.

This report uses formal hypothesis testing to evaluate whether a statistically significant difference exists between the mean monthly wages of male and female workers in Colombia's formal economy. The analysis is grounded in publicly available data from DANE's Gran Encuesta Integrada de Hogares (GEIH) and contextualized using institutional and academic sources.

Research Question

Is the mean monthly wage of male workers in Colombia's formal economy significantly higher than the mean monthly wage of female workers at a 5% significance level?

Methodology

A one-tailed (right-tailed) z-test for the difference in means is conducted, comparing the male group (benchmark) and the female group (test group). The test is appropriate given the large sample sizes drawn from DANE's GEIH 2023 dataset, which satisfies the Central Limit Theorem conditions. Following standard econometric practice (ILO, 2022), wages are measured in thousands of Colombian pesos (COP) per month.

Null and Alternative Hypotheses:

H₀: μ_male − μ_female = 0    (No significant difference in mean wages)
H₁: μ_male − μ_female > 0    (Male mean wage is significantly higher)

The parameters used in this test are:

  • Test direction: Right-tailed (one-tailed)
  • Significance level: α = 0.05
  • Male workers: x̄₁ = 1,842 (thousand COP), σ₁ = 920, n₁ = 1,200
  • Female workers: x̄₂ = 1,510 (thousand COP), σ₂ = 780, n₂ = 1,050
  • Observed difference: x̄₁ − x̄₂ = 332 thousand COP
Results

The z-test was performed using the interactive hypothesis testing tool developed for this assignment (C8/C9 modes, accessible at www.webpageGlobal_MartinezSara.com). The standard error of the difference was computed as:

SE = √( σ₁²/n₁ + σ₂²/n₂ ) = √( 920²/1200 + 780²/1050 ) ≈ 35.94 thousand COP

z = (x̄₁ − x̄₂) / SE = 332 / 35.94 ≈ 9.24

The key statistical outcomes are:

  • Test statistic: z = 9.24
  • p-value: p < 0.0001
  • Critical value (α = 0.05, right-tailed): z_c = 1.645
  • Decision: Reject H₀

Since the observed z-statistic (9.24) far exceeds the critical value (1.645) and the p-value is practically zero (p < 0.0001), there is overwhelming statistical evidence to reject the null hypothesis.

Discussion

The rejection of H₀ provides strong statistical support for the existence of a gender wage gap in Colombia's formal economy. According to DANE (2023), the average female worker in Colombia earns approximately 18–22% less than a comparably qualified male counterpart, a figure consistent with the 332 thousand COP gap modeled in this analysis. The ILO (2022) notes that such gaps are driven by a combination of occupational segregation, unpaid care responsibilities disproportionately borne by women, and direct wage discrimination.

The World Bank (2021) further highlights that although Colombia has ratified international conventions on equal pay (ILO Convention No. 100), enforcement mechanisms remain weak, particularly in informal labor markets which represent roughly 60% of Colombian employment. This means the wage gap measured in the formal sector likely underestimates the true disparity in total household income.

Contextual interpretation: The result is not merely statistically significant — it is economically meaningful. A 332,000 COP monthly gap represents a substantial share of median formal wages, affecting long-term wealth accumulation, pension savings, and household economic resilience for Colombian women (DANE, 2023).

Error analysis:

  • Type I Error (α = 0.05): There is a 5% probability of incorrectly concluding that a wage gap exists when in fact none exists. In this context, a Type I error would lead to unnecessary policy interventions and could misallocate public resources toward wage-equity programs that are not needed.
  • Type II Error (β): The probability of failing to detect a real wage gap when one exists. Given the extremely high z-statistic and near-zero p-value, the power of this test is essentially 1 (100%), making a Type II error virtually impossible with this sample. However, in smaller samples (e.g., regional sub-analyses), Type II errors could cause real disparities to be overlooked, delaying targeted policy responses.
Conclusion

At the 5% significance level, the hypothesis test provides strong, statistically conclusive evidence that male workers in Colombia's formal economy earn significantly more than female workers (z = 9.24, p < 0.0001). The null hypothesis is rejected in favor of the alternative. This result is consistent with the empirical literature on gender wage gaps in Latin America and underscores the need for continued structural reforms, including transparent pay auditing mechanisms, accessible childcare infrastructure, and stronger enforcement of equal-pay legislation (ILO, 2022; World Bank, 2021).
